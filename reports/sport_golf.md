**What survives, in plain English**
- G2: after round 2 and after round 3, back whoever is leading the tournament (all of them if tied) in the winner market, in the 3 hours before the next round, and hold it. On the recorded weeks the market gave those leaders a 27% chance of winning and they won 62% (13 bets, 6 tournaments). On its own that is about 1 in 1,000 by luck, but about 60 versions were looked at, so a result this strong turns up by luck roughly 1 time in 20. At the price actually on offer (average 5.2, £40 a bet) it kept +£159 per £100: first half +74, second +232, worst day +£8, +£303 without the best tournament. Including the night after round 1: 21 bets, +257 per £100, but the first half was only +18. The opposite side (laying the leader) loses. 13 bets is a sign, not proof; that is what the paper trial is for.

**Map** (the_map.md had no golf; this is new). Every recorded market, before the tournament, before each round and 2 hours into each round, three periods, against 100 simulations of luck: 9 cells pass against 7 expected by luck. All are long shots (rated 0.2-3%) that never won; using them means laying at 30.0 or longer, which the rules forbid. Standings after each round, against the price: players 4+ strokes outside a top-5/10/20 line finished there less often than the price said (top 20: 13% rated, 3% happened); again only usable as a long-odds lay.
- Trading out does not pay: backing before round 1 and closing after round 1, 2 or 3 lost 15-42 per £100 in every market and every target/stop setting (targets +25/50/100%, stops 30/60%, time limits round 3/4). The gap between back and lay costs more than the moves earn.

**Small markets**
- Money matched: three-balls a median £17 the day before and £124 in the last hour; match bets £300-400; top 5/10/20 £20-40k; winner £200k+.
- Match-bet favourites (G1, dropped): the first measure, +17 per £100 in both halves, came from mixing rounds. Betfair moves a match bet's start to each round. Measured round by round, before the tournament it kept +21.6 per £100 on 48 bets, but the first half lost (-2.4).
- Three-ball favourites with under £100 matched: +15.9 per £100, but the first half -19.5. With £100-1,000 matched they lose (-22).
- Side markets against the main market at the same moment: where a player's top-N price disagrees with his winner price, results follow the side market. No prices are left behind.
- Matchbook against Betfair (today only): Matchbook sometimes offered a back above Betfair's lay for the same player (Alex Noren 25.0 against 24.0 to lay, for at least 8 minutes). About £1.50 per £40, a few times a day. Smarkets showed no crosses. Needs a week of recording before it counts.
- Taking prices on outsiders in outrights loses 25-99 per £100. Any money there is in offering prices, which 15-minute quotes cannot test.

**Form guide:** each player's strokes beaten against the field in every round since 2020, adjusted for the strength of that field, weighted towards the last 240 days, fitted on 2022-25 only and tested on Sep-Oct 2026 (golf.form_rating, 135,133 rows). Before the tournament it is about level with the market: slightly better for top 20, slightly worse for top 5/10, the same for winner and three-balls. In three-balls the higher-rated player won 38% against 41% rated. Where form says a player's chance is twice the market's or more, backing him paid in neither half. In running (leaders after a round), form did not sort who held on. £0 a day; the known rules stay on file.
- Known rules run inside each golf type (drift, weight of money, favourite/outsider, the star, tour, round, money matched, dutching): about 250 cuts; 3 pay in both halves in one type, about what luck gives. Weight of money in round-2 three-balls: +48 per £100, 18 bets. Top-5 players with the lay side 2x+ the back side: +28, 51 bets, 4 tournaments. Three-ball outsiders with £100-1k matched: +23, but negative without the best tournament. Re-check due Mon 7 Dec on new recordings.
