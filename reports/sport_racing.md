**What the data does support (leads, not survivors):**
- **The starting price is right.** In every type of race the favourite wins as often as its starting price says (hurdles 37.6% rated / 37.5% won; flat turf 33.5 / 32.9; Ireland 34.1 / 33.6). Backing everything at the starting price loses about the commission. The draw is already in the price too: 1 of 153 course × distance × draw cells passes, where luck alone would give about 4.
- **Big fields:** in races with 16 or more runners the favourite was rated 24.5% and won 28.4% over 796 races (+£18.50 per £100 at the starting price). But the first period lost (−£3.00), and most of the gain is 2025 (+£55). The best version is big flat-turf fields (+£30 per £100, 381 races), which are summer races: only 23 are on our recorded days. **NOT YET: re-test at real prices when big-field flat turf returns (Lincoln meeting, late March 2027). Jumps races with 16+ runners will be checked from the recorder on Mon 7 Dec.**
- **Chases, horses priced 2 to 3:** backing at the starting price made +£5.30 per £100 on 872 bets, positive in all three periods (+6.7 / +6.5 / +2.4), but that is not beyond chance. On the recorded days, backing the same horses 10 seconds to 5 minutes before the off made +£17 to +£25 per £100 on about 50 races, positive in both halves. Its neighbours (priced 3 to 4) lose £8.80. Frozen as a lead in racing.candidates. **NOT YET: re-measure Mon 7 Dec with 10 more days.**
- **Starting-price trades (Peter's 21 Nov idea), measured on 633 races:** laying 10 minutes to 10 seconds before the off and closing at the starting price is break-even (−£1.70 to +£1.20 per £100). The gap between back and lay costs about what the price drift gives back. Backing and closing at the starting price is the same (−£2.30 to +£0.20). This is the cheapest way in and out, but there is nothing to collect from the move itself. Near and far projected prices against the final starting price are recording from today; the first read is due on Tue 1 Dec after four days of races.
- **In running, resting orders get picked off.** Over 5 years, backing at the starting price and leaving a lay in running at half the price lost £19 per £100 on favourites and £47 on outsiders. Horses only trade that low when the people with faster pictures already know they will win. The mirror image is the same: a favourite whose price doubles in running wins 8.3% of the time when the doubled price says 14.5% (6,817 races). Taking the price on offer, with the 1-second delay, does no better. Backing in running and trading out at a 5–20% target with a stop and a time limit ran from −£16 to +£5 per £100 across 100 versions, and the few small positives are about what luck gives. Laying the drifting favourite ran from −£60 to +£12, never positive in both halves. Backing it lost in 5 of 6 versions. The one that made money (back the favourite at 5–10 after its price doubles: 79 races, +£25.70, both halves) has losing neighbours on both sides, so it is noise.
- How each type behaves in running: sprints and all-weather races last 70–90 seconds and the favourite's price moves more than 5% in 56–61% of five-second windows. Three-mile chases last about 285 seconds and are calmer (31%). Only these slow races leave room for a five-second trade, and on the recorded days it still loses.

**Small markets.** British and Irish win markets are not small: a median of £200,000 is matched before the off, and only 0.08% of 14,909 races had under £5,000. The thin markets are the side markets: match bets, without-the-favourite and forecasts have a median of £0 matched, each way £2,100, other place terms £8,900, place £12,600. Their prices are posted by quoters who track the win market. At the same second, their place and other-place prices were closer to what happened than my own conversion of the win prices (other place, rated by the quote 67%, happened 70%). Over 171 races, taking any price that looked better than the win market lost £1.70 to £14 per £100, with one exception. Backing a place long shot (median price 13) when the win market made it 20% better value made +£23.90 per £100 on 65 bets, positive in both halves. But it came at an average stake of £6.70, because that is all that was on offer, and one day gave £193 of the £227. It is a lead: about £4 a day. I looked for side prices left behind after the win market moved 10% (the side quote unchanged since its last snap). There were only 23–28 cases: too few to judge (first half −£60, second half +£130). **NOT YET: re-run Mon 7 Dec.** The place market against the win market at the starting price, over 6,495 races: the place market is the right one, so there is nothing to take there. Forced trading in the last seconds: backing 10 seconds before the off against the starting price comes out level (about −£0.60 per £100 on horses priced under 3). £ a day at £2–£50 stakes: none positive yet.
