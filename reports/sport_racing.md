**What the data does support:**
- **The starting price is right, in every type.** The favourite going into the off wins as often as its starting price says: hurdles 37.3% rated / 37.6% won, chases 34.8 / 35.0, flat turf 33.1 / 32.3, Ireland 33.7 / 32.8. Backing at the starting price loses about the commission. The facts are already in the price. The draw: 1 of 153 course × distance × draw cells passes, where luck alone gives about 4. Jockey and trainer form (42,228 runners, 2024–26): 0 of 149 pass, where luck gives about 4. One weak sign: runners from trainers in hot form are over-backed (−£12.20 per £100 backing them); laying them under 5.0 made +£4.30, positive in both halves, but that is not beyond chance.
- **Chases, horses priced 2 to 3 (the one lead):** backing at the starting price made +£3.00 per £100 on 914 bets, positive in all three periods (+0.9 / +5.6 / +3.0), but not beyond chance. On the recorded days, backing them at the price on offer 10 seconds to 5 minutes before the off (counted back from the actual off) made +£17 to +£25 per £100 on about 50 races, positive in both halves. Their neighbours (priced 3 to 4) lose. Frozen in racing.candidates. **NOT YET: re-measure on Mon 7 Dec with 10 more days.**
- **Starting-price trades (Peter's 21 Nov idea), 633 races:** laying 10 minutes to 10 seconds before the off and closing at the starting price is break-even (−£1.70 to +£1.20 per £100). Backing and closing at the starting price is the same (−£2.30 to +£0.20). The gap between back and lay eats what the drift gives. Entering at the starting price is the cheapest way in, and the morning market is no better: backing the morning favourite at morning prices lost £3.60 per £100 against £1.90 at the starting price, and outsiders lost £10.50 against £1.70. **Projected starting prices:** on the first 18 races (Australia, horses and greyhounds) the near price was a worse guide to the final starting price than the best back on offer (median error 18% against 12%), and it called the direction about half the time. Layers' money waiting at the starting price was about 9 times the backers'. Too few races yet; British races tonight.
- **In running, resting orders get picked off.** Over 5 years, backing at the starting price and leaving a lay in running at half the price lost £19 per £100 on favourites and £47 on outsiders. Horses only trade that low when people with faster pictures know they are winning. The mirror is the same: a favourite whose price doubles to 5–10 in running wins 8.3% of the time when that price says 14.5% (6,817 races). Taking the price on offer with the 1-second delay does no better. Backing and trading out at a 5–20% target with a stop and a time limit ran from −£16 to +£5 per £100 over 60 versions, and the few small positives are what luck gives. Laying the drifting favourite ran from −£60 to +£12, never positive in both halves. Backing it after a setback lost in 5 of 6 versions.
- How each type behaves in running: sprints and all-weather races last 70–90 seconds, and the favourite's price moves more than 5% in 56–61% of five-second windows. Three-mile chases last about 285 seconds and move that much in 31%. Only these slow races leave room for a five-second trade, and on the recorded days it still loses.

**Small markets.** British and Irish win markets are not small: a median of £213,000 is matched before the off, and only 0.08% of races had under £5,000. The thin markets are the side ones: match bets, without-the-favourite and forecasts have a median of £0 matched, each way £2,100, other place terms £8,900, place £12,600. Their prices are posted by quoters who track the win market, and they track it well. Over 136 races, the quotes' own chances were closer to what happened than my conversion of the win prices (other place: quote 67%, happened 70%). Taking every side price that looked better than the win market at the same second lost in 21 of 25 versions; the 4 small positives each fail a half. Side prices left behind after the win market moved 10%: 1 to 19 cases a version, too few to judge. The place market against the win market at the starting price (6,495 races): the place market is the right one. Forced trading in the last seconds: backing 10 seconds before the off against the starting price comes out level (−£0.60 per £100 on horses under 3.0). £ a day at £2–£50 stakes: none positive yet.
